Winter picks: warmth where you actually sit

Heating a whole house to fix one cold desk is expensive. These are the targeted fixes — heat the person, not the room — plus the light and humidity that short days quietly take away.

Two things go wrong every winter: you get cold in one spot, and the air gets dry enough to wreck sleep and skin. The cheap fixes are a heated throw, a humidifier and a decent lamp — in that order. Space heaters come last because they cost the most to run.

When to actually buy

Timing beats loyalty in this category — the same product can move 30% across a year.

Best months to buy

Late January to March. Clearance on heaters and throws is deep once retailers stop stocking winter goods — but sizes and colours vanish first.

What to skip

Any heater that claims to heat a whole home from one socket. Physics does not allow it, and the safety margin on cheap units is thin.

How to spot a fake discount

Winter gear is heavily marked up in the first cold snap so the 'discount' restores the normal price. Check a price-history tool before buying in November.

Before you order

  • For heaters, check for tip-over and overheat cut-offs — non-negotiable in a small room.
  • Humidifiers need descaling; confirm the tank opening is wide enough to clean properly.
  • For anything electric used at a desk, check the cable length — most are shorter than people expect.

Questions people ask

Is a heated throw cheaper than heating the room?

Yes, by a wide margin. Warming a person directly uses a fraction of the energy of raising a room's air temperature.

Do SAD lamps actually help?

Bright-light lamps are a well-studied approach for seasonal low mood, but they are not a medical treatment. If symptoms are significant, talk to a clinician rather than buying a lamp.

Space heater or heated blanket first?

Heated blanket, almost always. It is cheaper to run, safer in small rooms and warms you faster.
