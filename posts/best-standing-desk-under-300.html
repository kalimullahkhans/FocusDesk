What to skip in this price range

  • Single-motor frames. The lift is slower, the load is uneven, and the wobble complaints are consistent. Not worth the $30 saving.
  • No-name frames with no warranty page. If the listing has no visible support contact, assume the motor is unserviceable.
  • Desks with the top pre-attached at a fixed width. You lose the ability to swap to a larger top later.
  • "Gaming" branding surcharges. Same frame, RGB tax.

Cheaper alternative: keep your desk, add a converter

Frequently asked

Can you get a decent standing desk for under $300?

Yes. You give up stability at full height, motor speed, and warranty length — but dual motors, memory presets, and 2–3 stage legs are all available in this tier now.

Single or dual motor?

Dual, always. Single-motor frames lift unevenly and are the main cause of wobble complaints below $300.

Cheap desk or a converter?

Solid desk you already like → converter. Small or wobbly desk → put the money into the desk; a converter amplifies existing wobble.

How much weight will it hold?

Most budget dual-motor frames are rated 120–180 lb (55–80 kg) — fine for two monitors and a laptop, tight for a tower plus dual 32-inch displays.
