The short verdict: buy in this order — chair → desk → monitor height → light → cables → everything else. Roughly 80% of the benefit comes from the first three items, and everything after that is aesthetics or convenience.

Why order matters more than budget

Most people spend their first $500 on a desk and a monitor stand, then sit in a $60 chair for a year with shoulder pain. The hierarchy is not taste — it is exposure time. You touch the chair for eight hours, the desk for maybe four, the monitor arm for zero. Spend accordingly.

The three most common money-wasters

  • A desk lamp. Takes the corner of the desk, lights the room instead of your work, and creates glare on the screen. A light bar does the job in zero footprint.
  • An expensive webcam. Buyers upgrade the camera when the problem is a window behind them and a lens at chest height. Fix geometry before spending.
  • A second monitor too early. On a 48" top with no arm, the second screen forces you to sit at an angle — which is worse than one screen positioned properly.

Frequently asked

What should I buy first?

Chair, then desk, then monitor height, then light. The chair is the only item you touch for eight hours a day.

How much does a complete setup cost?

About $700 for a genuinely usable one, $1,100–1,300 for balanced, $1,800–2,000 for complete. Above that you are buying aesthetics.

Where do people waste money?

A desk lamp instead of a light bar, an expensive webcam when the fix is lighting and height, and a second monitor before the first is positioned correctly.
